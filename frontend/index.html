StayStacking

StayStacking helps endurance athletes build fitness without breaking down. It tracks your training load so you can stack healthy weeks, not injuries.

Getting Started

Sync Hit the Sync button to pull your last 8 weeks of activities from Strava.
HR Zones Heart rate zones are pulled from your Strava profile. Update them in Strava's settings, then hit Sync to refresh.
Pace Zones Enter a recent race result on the Pace Zones view to derive your zones (VDOT). You can also set a custom pace to see time spent above or below a threshold.
Power Zones Enter your cycling FTP on the Power Zones view, then re-sync to see time spent in each power zone.
Log Activity Use the Activity Log tab to manually log activities to Strava — handy for sessions your watch didn't record. They feed straight into your load charts.

About Load Tracking

Your weekly load is tracked across distance, elevation, time, and your heart rate, pace, power, and grade zones. Use the buttons above the chart to switch between views. Sudden week-over-week spikes are the primary warning sign of injury risk — a gradual, consistent build is the goal.

Training Load Index

Below the chart, the Load Index breaks your training into three categories — Aerobic, Muscular, and Structural — and tracks each one:

Fitness CTL — your long-term training load (42-day average). The base you've built.
Fatigue ATL — your short-term load (7-day average). How much recent work is weighing on you.
Form TSB — Fitness minus Fatigue. Positive means fresh, negative means fatigued.
Injury Risk ACWR — recent load vs. your baseline. Aim for the 0.8–1.3 sweet spot; above 1.5 is danger.

Per-Run Analysis

Pick any run from the last 14 days to see a heart-rate-over-time chart plus a mile-by-mile breakdown — pace, average heart rate, and elevation gain/loss for each mile.
